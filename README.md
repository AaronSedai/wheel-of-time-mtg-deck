# The Wheel of Time × Magic: The Gathering

### An AI\-Assisted Commander Deck Design Project

This project is a complete 100\-card, *Wheel of Time*\-themed Magic: The Gathering Commander proxy deck created through iterative human\-AI collaboration.

The project began with a simple creative challenge: **Could the characters, locations, objects, and events of** ***The Wheel of Time*** **be translated into a cohesive and playable Magic: The Gathering Commander deck while preserving the identity of both worlds?**

I selected **Narset, Enlightened Exile** as the mechanical foundation for the Commander and reimagined the card as **Rand al'Thor, Dragon Reborn**. From there, I built a Jeskai (white, blue, and red) deck by evaluating existing Magic cards and matching their mechanics to characters, abilities, relationships, artifacts, locations, and events from *The Wheel of Time*.

Generative AI served as a collaborative design tool throughout the project. I defined the creative direction, selected and evaluated Magic mechanics, developed the card concepts, established visual requirements, refined prompts, reviewed generated results, identified inconsistencies, and iterated until the cards formed a unified 100\-card collection.

The finished project demonstrates not only AI\-assisted visual creation, but also **prompt engineering, requirements definition, iterative development, quality assurance, project organization, creative problem\-solving, and maintaining consistency across a large multi\-asset project.**

**Project scope:** 100 custom proxy cards

**Format:** Commander

**Color identity:** Jeskai (White / Blue / Red)

**Commander:** Rand al'Thor, Dragon Reborn *(Narset, Enlightened Exile)*

**Status:** Complete

## Design & AI Collaboration Process

Creating the deck required a repeatable workflow that combined human creative direction with generative AI. Each card moved through several stages before becoming part of the finished 100\-card collection.

### 1\. Concept & Thematic Mapping

I began by identifying characters, locations, artifacts, creatures, and important events from *The Wheel of Time* that could be represented within the deck.

Rather than inventing new Magic: The Gathering mechanics, I selected existing cards whose abilities could be meaningfully connected to each Wheel of Time concept. This established a mechanical foundation while allowing the thematic identity of the deck to develop around it.

### 2\. Mechanical Evaluation

Each selected Magic card was evaluated for how well its mechanics reflected the corresponding Wheel of Time character or concept while remaining compatible with the deck's Jeskai color identity.

This required balancing two goals:

- Preserve recognizable Magic: The Gathering gameplay.
- Make the resulting card feel thematically appropriate to *The Wheel of Time*.

The Commander established the model for this approach: **Narset, Enlightened Exile** provided the mechanics, while **Rand al'Thor, Dragon Reborn** provided the thematic identity.

### 3\. Visual Requirements & Prompt Development

Before generating artwork, I defined requirements for each card, including character appearance, clothing, setting, magical effects, important objects, environmental conditions, composition, and overall visual tone.

Prompts became increasingly structured as the project developed. Existing cards were used as visual references so that newly generated assets could preserve the established frame, typography, iconography, color language, and overall Wheel of Time aesthetic.

### 4\. AI\-Assisted Generation

Generative AI was used as a collaborative visualization tool to turn the established requirements into card artwork.

The AI generated candidate images based on the supplied specifications, while I remained responsible for the project's creative direction, thematic decisions, mechanical selections, and acceptance criteria.

### 5\. Evaluation & Iteration

Generated images were reviewed against the original requirements.

When an image contained an incorrect detail, inconsistent layout, unsuccessful composition, incorrect collector number, inappropriate visual element, or deviation from the established design language, I revised the requirements and generated another iteration.

Some cards required multiple revisions before meeting the project's standards.

### 6\. Consistency Across the Deck

As the collection grew, maintaining consistency became increasingly important.

Recurring elements such as card frames, typography, banners, mana\-color treatments, collector numbering, symbols, environmental composition, and artistic style were treated as shared design requirements rather than independently reinvented for every card.

The Basic Land cycles became an especially useful consistency exercise. Dragonmount, Tar Valon, and the Field of Merrilor each maintain a recognizable core composition while exploring different seasons, weather conditions, lighting, and historical or atmospheric contexts.

### 7\. Final Review & Asset Organization

Approved cards were assigned collector numbers from **001/100 through 100/100**, organized by card type, and preserved as the final assets for the playable proxy deck.

The completed collection was then organized in this repository so that the design process and finished work could be presented as a cohesive portfolio project.

## Visual Case Study: Dragonmount

One of the project's clearest examples of iterative AI\-assisted design is the **Dragonmount Basic Land cycle**.

Rather than generating ten unrelated Mountain illustrations, I established Dragonmount as the consistent visual subject for cards **071/100–080/100**. The mountain, surrounding landscape, Wheel of Time card frame, Mountain mana treatment, and overall composition served as shared design requirements across the cycle.

Individual cards were then developed by changing specific environmental variables while preserving that established visual identity. These variations explored differences in time of day, weather, season, atmosphere, and lighting.

This created an additional challenge for generative AI: **change what is requested without unnecessarily changing what has already been approved.**

During iteration, prompts increasingly emphasized requirements such as:

- Preserve the established Dragonmount silhouette and composition.
- Preserve the existing card frame and visual aesthetic.
- Modify only the requested environmental conditions.
- Maintain consistent Basic Land formatting.
- Preserve collector numbering and footer conventions.
- Review generated results and regenerate assets when requirements were not satisfied.

The resulting cycle demonstrates how progressively refined prompts and explicit constraints can help maintain visual continuity across a collection of related AI\-assisted assets.

## Project Challenges & Lessons Learned

Building a cohesive 100\-card collection revealed several challenges that were not apparent when the project began. Many of the most valuable lessons came from learning how to communicate requirements to generative AI, evaluate the resulting output, and maintain consistency as the project expanded.

### Maintaining Consistency at Scale

Creating one successful image was considerably easier than maintaining a recognizable visual system across 100 separate assets.

As the deck grew, previously approved decisions became requirements for future cards. Card frames, title treatments, mana colors, typography, collector numbers, symbols, layout, and overall artistic style all needed to remain recognizable throughout the collection.

**Lesson learned:** Large AI\-assisted projects benefit from treating established creative decisions as reusable specifications rather than redefining the desired result for every generation.

### Controlling the Scope of a Revision

One recurring challenge was requesting a small modification without unintentionally changing other successful elements of an image.

A request as simple as changing weather, clothing, a collector number, or one piece of card text could result in unrelated changes elsewhere in the generated asset.

Over time, revision instructions became more explicit, using language such as **"preserve the artwork," "preserve the card layout,"** and **"change one thing."**

**Lesson learned:** Effective prompting is not only about describing what should change. Clearly identifying what **must not change** can be equally important.

### Translating Creative Ideas into Testable Requirements

Early concepts often began as broad ideas: a character, location, magical event, or particular atmosphere.

Before generation, those ideas needed to become concrete visual requirements involving composition, clothing, environment, lighting, color, objects, magical effects, card mechanics, and formatting.

This transformed subjective creative direction into criteria that could be evaluated against a generated result.

**Lesson learned:** Better\-defined requirements made it easier to determine whether an AI\-generated result actually satisfied the intended goal.

### Recognizing When to Regenerate

Not every generation was successful.

Some outputs contained incorrect text, incorrect collector numbers, incomplete layouts, altered compositions, or visual details that did not match the established project requirements.

Rather than forcing an unsuccessful result into the collection, those outputs were treated as iterations. Requirements were clarified and the asset was generated again.

**Lesson learned:** Generative AI output should be reviewed rather than automatically accepted. Human evaluation and quality control remained essential throughout the project.

### Prompting Became Iterative

There was rarely a single "perfect prompt."

Successful assets often emerged through a sequence of instructions in which the result of one generation revealed what needed to be clarified in the next.

This changed the way I approached AI interaction. Instead of treating prompting as a one\-time command, I began treating it as an iterative design conversation.

**Lesson learned:** Working effectively with generative AI often resembles an iterative feedback loop: define, generate, evaluate, refine, and repeat.

### Organization Became Part of the Project

Once the collection approached 100 assets, file organization, collector numbering, card categories, naming conventions, and version awareness became important parts of completing the work.

The final collection was organized by card type and collector number before being transferred into this GitHub repository.

**Lesson learned:** Producing assets is only part of completing a large project. Organization and documentation determine whether those assets can be maintained, reviewed, and presented effectively.

### Human Direction Remained Central

The most important lesson from the project was understanding the distinction between **AI generation and human direction**.

Generative AI provided visualization and iteration capabilities, but the technology did not independently determine the project's goals, select the Commander, establish the deck's color identity, choose the mechanical foundations, decide which Wheel of Time concepts belonged in the deck, evaluate whether an image was successful, or determine when an asset was finished.

Those decisions remained part of my role throughout the project.

**Lesson learned:** I found generative AI most useful as a collaborative tool when I provided clear intent, constraints, evaluation, and direction rather than expecting the technology to determine the finished product on its own.

## Learning Context & Skills Demonstrated

### Applying Formal AI Learning to an Independent Project

Development of this project coincided with my completion of the **Google AI Professional Certificate**.

Working on both simultaneously gave me an opportunity to apply AI concepts beyond individual course exercises. As I learned new approaches to prompting, planning, content creation, responsible AI use, and human evaluation, I was also working through similar challenges within a sustained independent project.

The deck became a practical environment for reinforcing those lessons. Rather than using generative AI for isolated tasks, I needed to maintain requirements and creative continuity across a collection of 100 related assets.

This helped transform concepts I was learning into habits I could apply repeatedly throughout a real project.

### Skills Demonstrated

**Generative AI Collaboration**

Used generative AI as an iterative creative partner while maintaining responsibility for project direction, requirements, evaluation, and final asset selection.

**Prompt Development & Refinement**

Developed increasingly structured prompts that distinguished between elements that should change and previously approved elements that needed to remain consistent.

**Requirements Definition**

Translated creative ideas into specific requirements involving mechanics, composition, characters, environments, clothing, objects, lighting, card layout, typography, and visual continuity.

**Output Evaluation & Quality Assurance**

Reviewed generated assets against defined requirements and rejected or regenerated outputs containing incorrect text, collector numbers, incomplete layouts, inconsistent artwork, or other deviations.

**Iterative Problem\-Solving**

Used unsuccessful or incomplete outputs as feedback for refining subsequent instructions rather than treating the first generated result as final.

**Creative and Mechanical Mapping**

Connected characters, locations, artifacts, events, and themes from *The Wheel of Time* with existing Magic: The Gathering mechanics while maintaining the deck's established Jeskai design.

**Consistency Management**

Maintained shared visual conventions across a 100\-card collection, including framing, typography, iconography, color treatments, collector numbering, and recurring environmental compositions.

**Project Organization**

Structured final assets by card type and collector number and organized the completed collection for presentation and future maintenance.

**Git & GitHub Documentation**

Used GitHub to organize project assets, maintain a versioned project repository, document the development process, and create a portfolio\-ready presentation of the completed work.

### Key Takeaway

The project reinforced one principle more than any other:

**AI was most effective when treated as a collaborator within a human\-directed process, not as a replacement for creative judgment.**

The quality of the finished work depended on clearly defining the goal, communicating constraints, evaluating the result, identifying where it failed to meet expectations, and deciding what should happen next.
